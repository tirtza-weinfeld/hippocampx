
# Introduction


## Resizable Table Of Contents

## Step 1 — Given a vector field


$$
u:\mathbb{R}^d\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^d,\quad (x,t)\mapsto u_t(x)
$$
This is a rule: for any position $x$ and parameter $t$, it returns a velocity vector $u_t(x)$.
Before any ODE, we only have this "velocity everywhere" map.

## Step 2 — Introduce an unknown trajectory

We now **look for a function**
$$
X:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^d,\quad t\mapsto X_t
$$
This $X$ is the particle's path through space. At this point, $X$ is unknown — it could be any curve.

## Step 3 — Impose the motion rule (ODE)

At parameter value $t$, the particle is at $X_t$. The field assigns velocity $u_t(X_t)$.
The trajectory's instantaneous velocity is $\frac{d}{dt}X_t$. Impose equality:
$$
\frac{d}{dt}X_t = u_t(X_t).
$$

## Step 4 — Add an initial condition

We specify where the trajectory starts:
$$
X_0 = x_0.
$$
So the full ODE problem is:
$$
\frac{d}{dt}X_t = u_t(X_t),\quad X_0=x_0.
$$

## Step 5 — Expand the derivative definition

$$
\frac{d}{dt}X_t=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{X_{t+h}-X_t}{h}.
$$
So the ODE means:
$$
\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{X_{t+h}-X_t}{h}=u_t(X_t).
$$

## Step 6 — Replace the limit by a small step (Euler idea)

A computer cannot take $h\to 0$, so we choose a small fixed $h$ and approximate:
$$
\frac{X_{t+h}-X_t}{h}\approx u_t(X_t).
$$
Rearrange:
$$
X_{t+h}\approx X_t+h\,u_t(X_t).
$$

## Step 7 — Turn it into an iterative algorithm

Choose grid times $t_k:=kh$, and write $X_k\approx X(t_k)$.
Then the update becomes:
$$
X_{k+1}=X_k+h\,u_{t_k}(X_k),\quad X_0=x_0.
$$
