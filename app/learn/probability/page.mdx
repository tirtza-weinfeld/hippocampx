## 1. Central Tendency (The Center)

### 1.1 Mean (Dataset)
Average of **observed** samples.
$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n}$$
* **Example:** Data $\{1, 5\}$.
* **Calc:** $\frac{1+5}{2} = \mathbf{3}$

### 1.2 Expected Value (Discrete)
Theoretical average of a **random variable** with specific outcomes.
$$E[X] = \sum x P(x)$$
* **Example:** 50% chance of 1, 50% chance of 5.
* **Calc:** $(1 \cdot 0.5) + (5 \cdot 0.5) = \mathbf{3}$

### 1.3 Expected Value (Continuous)
Theoretical average of a **random variable** that can be any decimal.
$$E[X] = \int x f(x) dx$$
* **Example:** Any number between 1 and 5 (Uniform PDF $f(x) = 1/4$).
* **Calc:** $\int_{1}^{5} x \cdot \frac{1}{4} dx = \left[ \frac{x^2}{8} \right]_1^5 = \frac{25}{8} - \frac{1}{8} = \frac{24}{8} = \mathbf{3}$

## 2. Spread (Variance)

How far values deviate from the center. Squaring ensures positive distances and penalizes outliers.

### 2.1 Variance (Dataset)
Average of the squared deviations from the observed mean.
$$s^2 = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n}$$
* **Example:** Data $\{1, 5\}$, Mean = 3.
* **Calc:** $\frac{(1-3)^2 + (5-3)^2}{2} = \frac{4+4}{2} = \mathbf{4}$

### 2.2 Variance (Discrete)
Theoretical spread of a random variable.
$$Var(X) = \sum (x - \mu)^2 P(x)$$
* **Example:** 50% chance of 1, 50% chance of 5.
* **Calc:** $(1-3)^2 \cdot 0.5 + (5-3)^2 \cdot 0.5 = \mathbf{4}$

### 2.3 Variance (Continuous)
Theoretical spread across an infinite range of decimals.
$$Var(X) = \int (x - \mu)^2 f(x) dx$$
* **Example:** Any number between 1 and 5 (Uniform PDF $1/4$).
* **Calc:** $\int_{1}^{5} (x-3)^2 \cdot \frac{1}{4} dx = \mathbf{1.33}$

## 3. Generative Notation & Sampling

### 3.1 Random Variables
In generative modeling, data points (like images) are treated as vectors in high-dimensional space $\mathbb{R}^d$.
* **$X \sim p(x)$**: Variable $X$ is distributed according to (follows) the distribution $p(x)$.
* **$z \sim p_{data}$**: Sampling a real data point from the true world distribution.
* **$\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$**: Sampling "pure noise" from a Standard Gaussian.
* **i.i.d.**: Independent and Identitically Distributed. Samples are independent but from the same $p_{data}$.

### 3.2 Probability Distributions ($p$)
* **$p_{data}$ (Target)**: The complex distribution of real data (peaks at data, valleys elsewhere).
* **$p_{init}$ (Source)**: The starting noise distribution, usually a Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$.
* **$p_t$ (Probability Path)**: Time-dependent interpolation. $p_0 = p_{init}$ and $p_1 = p_{data}$.
* **$\delta_z$ (Dirac Delta)**: A "point-mass" distribution. Sampling from $\delta_z$ always returns $z$ (deterministic/zero variance).


### 3.3 Conditional Paths
* **$p_t(x|z)$**: The "Conditional Probability Path." Given the final destination is $z$, what is the probability of being at $x$ at time $t$?
* **Constraint**: $p_0(\cdot|z) = p_{init}$ and $p_1(\cdot|z) = \delta_z$.

## 4. Stochastic Processes (SDE)

Used when adding randomness to the transition (Diffusion).

* **$W_t$ (Brownian Motion)**: A continuous-time random walk where increments $W_t - W_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, (t-s)I_d)$.
* **$dW_t$**: An infinitesimal "nudge" of Gaussian noise.
* **$dX_t = u_t(x)dt + \sigma_t dW_t$**: 
    * $u_t(x)dt$ is the **Drift** (deterministic direction).
    * $\sigma_t dW_t$ is the **Diffusion** (stochastic jitter).

## 5. Operations & Summary

### 5.1 The Expectation (Average over Distributions)
In training, we minimize Loss using the **Expectation** operator $\mathbb{E}$:
$$\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_{data}, \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}} [ \| \text{model}(z, \epsilon) - \text{target} \|^2 ]$$
*Intuition: "On average, across all data and all noise, how far off is the model?"*
